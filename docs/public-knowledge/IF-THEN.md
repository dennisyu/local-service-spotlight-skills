# IF/THEN: the public recommendation layer

Local Service Spotlight's IF/THEN system turns a large body of training, interviews, case studies and operating experience into situation-specific guidance.

The core idea is simple:

> If a person is in a specific situation, show the most relevant supported next action, verify whether they did it, and learn from whether it worked.

This is closer to a useful conversation than a linear course.

## The public object

An IF/THEN recommendation should be understandable without private systems:

```
IF:      observable situation
THEN:    recommendation
WHY:     short rationale
SOURCE:  public evidence / canonical method
DO:      next smallest useful action
VERIFY:  receipt or measurable check
OUTCOME: larger goal
NEXT:    next branch
```

## Example: young-adult builder entry

**IF:** Someone says they want to learn digital marketing/AI and work with real businesses.

**THEN:** Start by applying in public rather than requesting private coaching.

**DO:** Watch a relevant Dennis Yu teaching/podcast video or Marky Mechanic video and post a roughly one-minute response explaining what was learned, what will be done, and what the person wants to become capable of owning.

**VERIFY:** Public post exists.

**NEXT:** A small revision/execution/teamwork gate before funded apprenticeship access.

The point is not the video itself. It is a cheap test of whether someone can consume instructions, act, communicate and accept feedback before scarce resources or client access are allocated.

## Example: support routing

**IF:** A learner asks for help.

**THEN:** Ask for structured evidence before turning the request into private troubleshooting.

A useful help packet states:

1. desired outcome;
2. SOP/checklist followed;
3. what was tried;
4. what happened;
5. evidence/receipt;
6. exact blocker or decision.

This makes support searchable and teachable.

## Example: local SEO

**IF:** A local-service business is not producing enough qualified calls from organic/local visibility.

**THEN:** Diagnose the actual visibility/conversion system before prescribing content volume.

Possible branch inputs include:

- category and geography;
- Google Business Profile state;
- branded vs non-branded visibility;
- review quality/velocity;
- website entity clarity;
- service/location coverage;
- call tracking;
- local links/citations;
- technical indexability;
- content/proof gaps;
- competitive SERP/Maps evidence.

The recommendation should point to the relevant canonical method and produce a checkable next action.

## Why public IF/THEN pages matter

They turn hidden expertise into:

- long-tail search pages;
- AI-citable answers;
- training steps;
- decision-support content;
- public proof of the method;
- a routing layer for leads;
- a shared memory for team members and agents.

## What should remain private

Public IF/THEN pages should not expose:

- private client data;
- private calls/transcripts;
- credentials;
- internal personnel notes;
- unpublished financials;
- unapproved likeness/media.

The public recommendation can cite the public canonical method while private evidence remains in the authorized execution system.

## Learning loop

The system should record:

- picked;
- committed;
- done;
- not applicable;
- goal reached;
- time to proof;
- support cost.

The best recommendation is not the one that gets the most clicks. It is the one that reliably moves the right person toward the desired outcome with defensible evidence and reasonable support cost.

## Relationship to Skool

Skool should provide the sequence, accountability and community around the public recommendation graph.

Public web:
- explains;
- demonstrates;
- attracts;
- gets cited;
- answers long-tail questions.

Skool:
- sequences;
- gates;
- tracks;
- coaches;
- certifies;
- connects people to projects/opportunities.

A lesson can point to the public canonical page, then require a private/public proof action to unlock the next stage.

## Relationship to Spotlight sites

A shared IF/THEN object can appear in different vertical views.

Example:

- HVAC Spotlight: slow-season HVAC trigger;
- Roofer Spotlight: storm-season roofing trigger;
- Pest Control Spotlight: route-density or seasonality trigger.

The underlying method may be shared while the evidence, vocabulary and recommended action are category-specific.
