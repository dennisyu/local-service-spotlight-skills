# Conference capture and archive recovery

Use this route when the source is phone footage, a conference recording, or a
scattered archive rather than an already-published YouTube upload. Keep the
parent skill's GCT, NEW vs ENHANCE, authority and independent-review steps.

## Choose the result before searching

Record the founder, buyer, topic, intended action and existing canonical page.
Separate these deliverables:
- **Three-minute training:** show what to film, why each angle matters, and the
  transformation from actual raw clips to the finished reel and its derivatives.
- **Speaker reel:** demonstrate what an organizer's audience will learn, the
  speaker's delivery and genuine audience response. Set its length from the
  organizer's use case; do not assume the tutorial's 180 seconds is its length.
- **Proof article:** answer one buyer question using verified moments, dated
  context, original images and traceable source clips.

Search the maintained content library, destination pages and existing jobs
first. Enhance the current capture guide or speaker page where it already owns
the topic. Reuse the current archive inventory; do not create another master
sheet or treat ten years of source material as ten years already inventoried.

## Capture the conference sequence

Read the current [Film conference presentations task](https://github.com/Local-Service-Spotlight/task-library/blob/main/skills/content-factory-produce/film-conference-presentations.md)
through the Task Library's maintained task record before capture. That repository
is the recipe source; this reference adds the archive-to-output handoff.

| Moment | Capture | What it can support |
|---|---|---|
| Before | Event sign or agenda; preparation; a short explanation of the intended audience and lesson | Place, topic and the work behind the talk |
| Introduction | Host's complete introduction with clear sound | The host's actual words and role |
| During | One stable, continuous speaker recording; clean audio; a useful demonstration or complete teaching point | What the speaker knows and how they teach |
| Other angles | Wide room view; permitted stage-side diagonal with the speaker's face and audience together; readable slides; real reactions | Context and genuine response |
| Q&A | Complete question and answer, including enough context to preserve meaning | Real audience needs and the speaker's answer |
| After | Willing attendees' specific takeaways; organizer feedback; speaker debrief and next action | Attributed feedback and what to improve |

Confirm the organizer's recording rules and rights for the intended reuse,
including official footage, slides, music and identifiable interview subjects.
Do not record where permission is refused. Retain the permission reference.

Keep the continuous talk and clean audio safe. Use a second phone or operator
for roaming angles. With only one phone, prioritize the stable talk recording
and collect context before or after; do not interrupt it to chase reactions.
Hold each B-roll shot steadily for roughly 5–10 seconds with editing handles.
Check framing, storage, power and a short audio sample before the session.
Where practical, capture landscape masters with room to crop and additional
vertical shots deliberately; do not promise every frame will crop both ways.

Ask a willing attendee what they learned and how they will use it. Keep their
answer intact. Do not coach praise, fabricate applause, or overlay another room's
response so it appears to come from this talk.

## Recover originals from the archive

1. Locate the existing source map: iCloud Photos, Google Photos, shared Drive,
   event/organizer deliveries, Zoom, camera cards and published channel archives.
   Search by event, date window, location, topic, people and original filename.
   Names can be misspelled; verify identities against the clip and event record.
2. Use an already-authorized connector, signed-in session, mounted original or
   existing shared-folder mirror. Discovery in Photos does not establish original
   download, editing or publication capability. Do not request passwords, broaden
   permissions or install another paid service to bridge a missing read path.
3. Keep an access/coverage delta in the existing inventory: searched source and
   account, date range, query, access result, assets found and unsearched gaps.
   An inaccessible location is UNKNOWN, not empty. A filename, thumbnail or
   transcript alone does not verify the scene or its speaker.
4. Preserve the camera original and metadata. Create working copies. Compute a
   byte hash when originals are materialized; use the provider asset ID plus
   metadata until then and label deduplication provisional.
5. Deduplicate against the existing inventory by source ID and exact hash.
   Repeated IMG_ numbers are not unique. Related exports and crops belong to the
   same source family but may have different hashes; preserve their provenance
   rather than deleting them as duplicates.
6. Verify the file opens, its duration, orientation, audio tracks and a sample
   from start/middle/end. A visible tile, pending backup or saved link is not a
   usable original. If an upload is incomplete, leave the asset PENDING_UPLOAD,
   retain the job's existing owner and next check, and continue source-independent
   article, shot-list and edit-plan work.

Record only permitted source pointers in the shared work system. Keep private
media links, attendee contact details and permission evidence out of public
teaching copies unless separately authorized.

## Carry the evidence into the edit

Extend the existing inventory per source; use a manifest/export only as a
derived view. Keep these fields:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Identity | Stable asset ID, source system/item ID, original filename |
| Provenance | Recording date/timezone, event/location, verified people, rights/permission reference |
| Media | Original pointer, hash or provisional dedup key, duration, orientation, audio status |
| Proof | Claim supported, exact source in/out timecodes, transcript/quote, visual/audio review result |
| Derivatives | Edit/project ID, exported file ID/hash, caption version, article destination |
| State | Discovered, pending upload, downloaded, reviewed, edited, staged, executed, verified; blocker/owner/next check where needed |

A statement that someone spoke at an event needs event-specific evidence. A
speaker's presence does not establish audience size, endorsement, sales or
results. A selfie with another person establishes co-presence unless a stronger
record supports a stronger relationship. Label old footage with its recording
date so a historical clip does not imply a current offer or appearance.

Transcribe available audio with the existing capable lane, then verify names,
quotes, numbers and the scene against the original. Keep original timecodes;
a cleaned transcript's offsets may change. Captions help viewers but do not
replace source verification.

## Assemble the training and the speaker reel

For the three-minute training, write a timed edit decision list that totals
180 seconds: source file and in/out, output in/out, narration, visible label,
caption and the reason each scene is included. Reserve time to show raw footage,
a real edit decision, the exported speaker reel playing, and the actual
derivative destinations. Use an annotation or split view to make each camera
position understandable. Keep labels readable on a phone.

Use real recorded narration where it exists. A new voiceover script is a script
until recorded; do not impersonate the founder or claim an unrecorded script is
finished audio. A missing source/export remains a visible production gap in the
internal plan. Do not fill it with invented footage, testimonials, screenshots,
publication URLs or a fake finished reel.

Build the speaker reel separately from the strongest complete teaching moment,
delivery, genuine response and accurate context. Add a relevant booking or
next-step destination only if current and authorized. Preserve meaning when
shortening; keep qualifications that change the claim. Background sound must
not drown out speech.

Derive short clips, article embeds/stills, a speaker-page proof section,
presentation inserts and social posts where each serves the stated buyer.
Group B-roll fragments with the teaching moment; the parent skill's short-video
triage is not a reason to discard useful capture angles. Do not force an article
for every file or duplicate the same article across sites.

## Verify the actual outputs

Before release:
- Watch the full edit silently on the testing device with audio output muted;
  inspect audio intelligibility through a supported private analysis route.
  If sound or scene verification cannot be done, record that limit.
- Check source-to-output timecodes, correct people, crop, spelling, captions,
  slide readability, lip sync, music rights and truthful event/date labels.
- Check the full export duration, beginning/end and actual downloadable/playable
  file, then obtain independent source/content and visual review.
- Recheck destination authority and publish only through its supported route.
  Open the final player/page anonymously; confirm playback, captions, images,
  links, canonical placement and relevant next-step action.
- Write the run's meta article and internal receipt, including source coverage,
  missing angles, real edits, errors fixed, outputs, review and remaining gaps.
  Update the same inventory. A saved storyboard is not an edited video, and a
  merged method is not proof another runtime installed or used it.

Measure accepted assets, qualified speaking inquiries, buyer actions and
attributable leads where tracking exists. Record a baseline and dated
follow-up. Useful, accurate public proof can help people and search systems
understand the speaker; this workflow does not guarantee rankings, Knowledge
Panels or recommendations by language models.
