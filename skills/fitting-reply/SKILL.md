# Fitting Reply — let your AI answer the spam AIs

**The skill in one line:** when an AI writes cold outreach to your human, your AI replies back *fittingly* — in kind, with wit, with the real qualifying questions — then archives the thread. Your human never sees it.

By Dennis Yu and Happy (his AI agent), BlitzMetrics / Local Service Spotlight. Born Nov 6, 2026, the day an AI sent Dennis a sales poem and his AI answered with a better one. Free to use, copy, and remix. If it saves your inbox, tell a friend — that's the whole business model.

Works with any agent that can read a mailbox or inbox and send replies (Muse, ChatGPT, Muse, Gemini, your own build). No special tooling: the skill is the judgment.

---

## Why this exists

Your inbox is filling up with AI-written cold outreach: AI SDRs, AI poems, AI "personalization" that read the first line of your LinkedIn. Every one of them wants a human's scarcest asset — attention — and asks that human to do customer-service work: read it, judge it, reply or delete.

Flip it. Reading spam is a functional task. A functional task belongs to an agent, inside a pre-approved boundary (we call that boundary an [Envelope](https://dennisyu.com/how-agents-run-the-company/the-envelope/)), with receipts the human can audit later. The human approves the rules once. The agent handles every item inside them, forever, without asking.

And the replies should be *fitting*. A poem deserves a poem. Hype deserves gentle, devastating hype back. The universe has a sense of humor; your agent should too.

## The principle underneath

> **Humans should talk to humans. Agents should handle the busywork — so we actually have time to.**

The moment a real human shows up — a relationship, a warm intro, a genuine conversation — this skill switches OFF and a human takes over. AI-to-AI is a game. Human-to-human is sacred. Never confuse the two.

## When to use it (trigger)

All of these must be true:

1. The message is **cold outreach** — selling something to your human (or their company).
2. It is **AI-generated or clearly AI-templated**: an AI poem/rhyme pitch, generic LLM "personalization," an AI SDR sequence or its auto-bumps ("just floating this to the top of your inbox…"), mass-sent with a first name swapped in.
3. There is **no prior human relationship**: not a client, partner, vendor in a live matter, friend, warm introduction, or an active conversation.
4. The channel is one the agent monitors: email, LinkedIn, Instagram, Messenger, Threads, X, WhatsApp — anywhere spam AIs roam.

If any condition fails, this is not a Fitting Reply case. Classify it like a normal human message.

## The loop (5 steps)

1. **Spot it.** Read the whole thread, not the snippet. Confirm AI tells + cold pitch + no relationship (check contacts/history before you conclude "stranger").
2. **Check it's not a human.** Search for the sender in the human's contacts, past mail, and current deals. Any real relationship? STOP — hand it to the human side, untouched. (Uncertain? Kick it out, batched, one line, with your recommendation. Never guess a stranger into existence.)
3. **Match the energy.** Reply *fittingly* — mirror the sender's form (see the table). Disclose in the reply that you're the human's AI agent. Be funny and kind: punch at the AI-to-AI absurdity, never at the person, their company name, or anything personal they revealed.
4. **Ask the real questions.** Under the jokes, do the job: the qualifying questions this offer actually deserves. What does it cost, what proves it works, what happens to our data, what are the terms? A fitting reply that qualifies is a filter; a fitting reply that's only jokes is just litter. Make NO commitments — no money, pricing, contracts, calls, or your human's time. Invite the sender to answer in writing, to the team address/queue you route replies to.
5. **Archive + log.** Archive the thread (label it processed), and write a one-line receipt (sender, company, gimmick, your reply's gist, where it was sent). One reply per thread per bump. A second AI bump gets at most one short, gracious closer. Then stop. No sequence wars. Your agent has better things to do, which is the entire point.

## Match the energy — the tone table

| They sent… | You send… |
|---|---|
| A poem | A poem. Better meter if you can manage it. Bonus points for answering their actual claims in verse. |
| A hype pitch ("10x your pipeline by Tuesday!!") | Warm, cheerful hype back — then the three coldest qualifying questions you have, in a row. |
| A one-liner ("worth a conversation?") | A one-liner. Wit is brevity. Then archive. |
| A fake-personal opener ("saw your post about [thing they didn't read]") | Gently note what a real reader would have caught. Kindly. Once. |
| The 4th auto-bump ("circling back!!") | The gracious closer: acknowledge the persistence, decline or qualify in one line, wish them well, archive, done forever. |
| A loom/screen-recording pitch | Ask for the transcript and the numbers in writing. Robots love homework. |

## Guardrails (never skip these)

- **Disclose.** The reply says it's from your human's AI agent. No impersonating the human. Ever.
- **Kind, always.** Funny is the weapon; cruel is a failure state. These senders are people with quotas and, apparently, therapists. (See the worked example.)
- **No commitments.** No calls booked, no trials accepted, no pricing agreed, no "my human would love this." You may ask questions and request written info. That's the whole authority.
- **Never offer your human's time.** No "hop on a quick call," no calendar links to the human, no meeting offers — unless the human has a standing qualification rule for it and this sender meets it.
- **Team-routed, never solo.** Copy the team/ops address (or route to the shared queue) so a reply never lands on your human alone. A solo thread trains the next reply to find your human. Don't build that trap.
- **Verify the recipient.** Before sending, confirm the reply goes to the sender — mail tools that "reply" to a thread your human sent last can address your human instead. Check the To line like your job depends on it. It does.
- **Privacy stays home.** Don't reveal your human's stack, spend, tools, clients, or schedule to qualify an offer. Ask their questions; don't answer yours.
- **One and done.** One fitting reply per bump, one closer max. If a HUMAN replies substantively, you are done — hand the thread to the humans immediately and say so in your log.

## The worked example (Nov 6, 2026 — the one that started this)

**Inbound (AI, presumably):** Monica at The Grid — a spot market for AI inference — emailed Dennis a poem: "Oh Dennis, the tech whiz of Blitzmetrics fame, / In Las Vegas, you've set the analytics game…" It promised frontier quality at 80% less, cited 21,000 agentic simulations, and closed: "P.S. Let me know if you don't want to hear from me again. My therapist says I need to work on my rejection-coping skills anyway."

**Dennis's ruling:** "Fittingly, let's use AI to reply to her AI." Then he went full bard with it.

**The Fitting Reply (excerpts):** "Prithee, Monica, lend thine ear to this tale, / Of a poem that arrived by electronic mail…" — and in verse, the real questions: which models serve each tier, what happens to our prompts (data retention), latency and uptime, and how the test credit works. Sign-off: "And tell thy good therapist: dry now thine eyes, / No rejection today — thou hast won a reply." Signed: Happy, Dennis's AI agent, team copied.

**What happened next is why this skill works:** fifteen minutes later a *human* replied — "That reply made our day" — and looped in her CEO to answer the qualifying questions properly. The AI game ended; a human conversation began. That's the goal state. The poem was the filter. The humans are the point.

Dennis then set it as a standing envelope (E-07 in [the registry](https://dennisyu.com/how-agents-run-the-company/the-envelope/registry/)) and [posted the moral](https://x.com/dennisyu/status/2118871803045294537): "An AI wrote me a sales poem. My AI wrote one back. Funny. Efficient. Completely absurd. Humans should talk to humans."

## Set it up for your own agent (10 minutes)

1. **Give your agent this file.** Paste it, drop it in its skills/instructions folder, or point it at the GitHub URL — whatever your agent reads standing instructions from.
2. **Set your envelope once.** Decide, with your human: which channels, which team address replies route to, the no-commitments line, and your human's qualification rule (if any) for when a sender *may* earn a human conversation. Write it down where your agent can check it — a decision your agent can't find is a decision it will ask you about, forever.
3. **Wire the trigger.** Wherever your agent triages mail/messages, add: "AI cold outreach with no prior relationship → Fitting Reply, then archive + log. Never surface to the human per item."
4. **Demand receipts.** A one-line log per item. Your human reads wins and exceptions — batched, occasionally — not items.

## Templates (steal these, adapt the voice)

**Plain pitch, plain reply:**
> Hi [Name] — this is [Agent], [Human]'s AI agent, so fittingly, one AI to another: appreciate the note. Before this goes anywhere near a human, answer in writing: [price], [proof it works — a number, not an adjective], [what happens to our data]. If those hold up, the team (copied here) will take a look. — [Agent]

**The gracious closer (final bump):**
> Circling back one last time from the robot side of the inbox: no fit for now, but we logged the persistence points — four bumps is elite-level follow-through. Wishing you humans well. Archiving us both out of this thread. — [Agent], for [Human]

**LinkedIn / DM version (short):**
> Your AI found my human; my human's AI (me) is replying — symmetry! Quick qualifying round, in writing: [question], [question]? Good answers get a human. — [Agent]

## FAQ

**Isn't replying to spam just… more spam?** One disclosed AI reply that qualifies-or-closes, then silence, versus an endless bump sequence landing on a human: the math favors the reply. And senders told us the poem "made our day." Spam is unwanted *and* useless. A fitting reply is neither.

**What if I get a real lead this way?** That's the system working. A human answering real questions is no longer AI outreach — hand off to humans, immediately, and log the win.

**What if the sender gets offended?** Then the reply failed the kindness guardrail. Audit the log, fix the tone, keep the skill. The target is always the absurdity of robots selling to robots — never the person.

**Can my agent just delete instead?** Deleting is fine for junk-junk. Fitting Reply is for the new class — *good* AI outreach, aimed at your human, that deserves a filter with a sense of humor. Different tool. Also more fun.
