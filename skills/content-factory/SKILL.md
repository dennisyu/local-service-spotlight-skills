# Content Factory

**Use this when** you've captured one thing — a Zoom interview, a 60-second hallway clip, a conference talk — and want a content library that compounds instead of a post that disappears. Step 8 of the Local Service Spotlight method, and the engine the other nine skills feed.

## Inputs
- One real raw recording (video or audio) plus a transcript. A clear phone recording can be sufficient; check intelligibility, rights and source provenance.
- The subject's positioning and proof (from `personal-brand-strategist` and `positive-mentions-harvester`).
- Your proof inventory — verified stories, appearances, praise, and evidence-bounded
  relationship terms. Rank records internally by Who / Where / What; do not use a
  recognizable name as borrowed status.
- Your canonical hub URLs, so nothing you publish competes with them.
- The existing entity/article inventory and its owners and holds. Identify the people, companies, concepts and products supported by the source, their SEO Tree parents, and any relevant current DIY and assistance paths. Reuse the inventory; do not start a second map.

Before processing, open the current [Article Guidelines](https://localservicespotlight.com/article-guidelines/) and the accepted [Nine Triangles reference](../nine-triangles/references/canonical-framework.md). Use Goals, Content, Targeting to choose the work; Learn, Do, Teach to capture the experience; Content, Checklist, Software to make the lesson repeatable; and Metrics, Analysis, Action to improve the next run. Keep the accepted triangle names and four factory stages intact.

## The factory line — Produce, Process, Post, Promote

Run the line in order. Each station feeds the next; skip one and the next breaks.

1. **Produce** — capture real source material: a recording, customer story, job,
   demonstration, review, or interview.
2. **Process** — transcribe, verify, and mine the source for claims, quotes, and complete
   proof moments: scene, meaning, person, true human beat, and compact receipt. Edit and
   atomize without inventing any part of them.
3. **Post** — assemble the one canonical hub and its channel-ready derivatives. Stage by
   default; publish only under the caller's exact action authority.
4. **Promote** — rank proven organic performers and prepare Dollar-a-Day recommendations.
   Live spend, kill, scale, and audience changes require a scoped approval receipt.

This factory sits inside the six-phase Social Amplification Engine. Plumbing comes
before the line; accepted Goals and Targeting aim it; weekly MAA reads performance.
Older material uses Plumbing, Publish, Promote, Perform as a wrapper for that broader
system. It is not a second name for the factory assembly line.

## The assembly line — six steps per recording
1. **Capture** — record once. One Zoom interview or one 60-second conference clip is a month of raw material.
2. **Transcribe & mine** — pull the strongest quotes, claims, numbers, and complete
   story moments out of the transcript. Preserve the scene and human beat around a quote;
   a disembodied famous name is not a story.
3. **Canonical article delta** — improve the existing hub through `definitive-article-writer`. Create a new hub only when no owner exists and the source supports a distinct reader need; a new recording alone is not a new topic. Preserve useful prior experience, add verified new evidence, connect parent, siblings and supporting proof, and provide the DIY and current assistance paths where relevant. Its first 2–3 sentences connect a true source moment or finding to the reader's situation, why it matters and the useful action or lesson. Apply the shared specific-GCT opening review to the hub and adapt it to each derivative's actual reader and format.
4. **Atomize** — cut clips, reels/shorts, and social posts from the same recording. Keep complete, useful moments and channel fit. Record actual accepted yield rather than promising a fixed asset count per recording.
5. **Distribute** — prepare channel-specific packages for YouTube/Shorts, LinkedIn,
   Instagram, TikTok, X, Facebook, and email. Publish/send only under exact action
   authority. Each relevant asset links back to its hub through the channel's supported format; never create a competing page. Carry the approved claim, source and useful next step into each package. Verify the native permalink and actual media/text after posting; staging, sending to a publisher and public delivery are separate states.
6. **Boost recommendation** — prepare the $1/day test for proven winners
   (`dollar-a-day-strategist`). Do not spend without the exact approval scope.

## The org chart — worker, manager, architect
- **Worker function** — cuts clips, prepares channel packages, and reports against the checklist.
- **Manager** — runs the workflow, enforces the SOP, reviews QA before anything publishes.
- **Architect** — designs the system: sets the Goals in GCT, picks topics off the topic wheel, approves the definitive article, owns the standards.

You are the architect. Build the factory; don't run the line. Capture and source verification need the subject's real experience; processing and distribution belong to qualified workers with the right inputs and authority. Write the SOP so another worker can repeat and check the result.

## First run — this week
1. Book one 30-minute Zoom where someone interviews YOU about your differentiation from step 1.
2. Run steps 2–5 on it; assign each step to an owner function with a deadline.
3. Stage the two strongest clips for a $1/day test. After a human approves the exact
   account, amount, and dates, read the MAA evidence at day 7. The agent recommends the
   decision; it does not autonomously spend, kill, or scale.

## Output
- One definitive article + a pack of clips, shorts, tweets, and ad-ready creative — all cross-linked to the hub.
- A written SOP per stage, so a worker runs the next recording without you.
- The source-backed delta applied to the existing entity inventory: canonical owner, related entities and proof, parent and useful sibling links, applicable DIY/assistance destinations, actual publication receipts and unresolved gaps. Do not copy private evidence into public articles or social posts.
- Cadence: capture monthly, atomize weekly, stage proven-winner tests, and review MAA
  weekly — adjust the system, not just the posts.

## Run in a supported persistent-agent runtime
- **Loop to done:** one recording in, all six steps checked — the run is not done at the
  definitive article; loop until the clips are cut, channel packages are ready, every
  asset points back to the hub, and proven candidates are queued for
  `dollar-a-day-strategist`. External execution remains a separate approved state.
- **Self-verify:** QA each stage against its SOP before the next stage starts — a deviation that ships compounds downstream.
- **Compound through the master:** read the maintained hub URLs, entity inventory and per-stage SOPs at each run. Apply new experience there and propagate affected articles, training and channel assets. An agent's memory is a working cache, not the only source another worker needs.
- **Log and replay the loop:** write the run's meta article with source, recipe revision, execution ID, actual results and the next receiving function. Review supported lessons into the recipe or standard, then retest the affected output. Check that another operator can use the exact revision before claiming adoption. Inspect existing schedules before creating another one; a written cadence or handoff does not prove a recurring run fired.

See `boil-the-ocean.md` for the full operating principles.

## Notes — Dennis's method
- Capture a substantive interview once, produce useful audience-specific derivatives, then consider amplification only after real engagement and exact spending approval.
- Keep the original recording, approved claims, derivative artifacts and observed performance linked. The workflow alone does not establish a customer or revenue result.
- Feature the moment, not the recognizable name. A clip earns attention when it carries a
  useful scene, lesson, or true human beat. A recognizable guest does not transfer
  authority by proximity. Rank eligible proof internally by Who / Where / What, then tell
  it without status signaling.
- Standardize and productize. Custom work doesn't scale and doesn't sell; a factory does. Boil the ocean on the system once, then let workers run it forever.
- The factory is the Publish-and-Promote muscle behind your entity home (step 4) and Knowledge Panel plumbing (step 5) — corroboration at scale.

## Operational lessons

These portable lessons describe checks and failure modes, not a report of a
particular account or client. Verify current vendor contracts before use.

- An incremental channel watcher should inventory the authorized upload source on its first run, retain a watermark and process only new items later. Reconcile deletions and failed items instead of assuming an empty feed means no work.

- Triage each recording against the caller's Goals, Content and Targeting before spending effort. Record SKIP, LIGHT, MODERATE or STRONG and the reason; use actual content evidence.

- Search the destination before writing. Enhance an existing canonical article when it owns the subject; do not create a competing page merely because a new recording arrived.

- If one recording legitimately serves a personal site and a company site, use distinct audiences and points of view with useful cross-links. Do not publish the same article twice.

- Run the content through the maintained writer and an independent reviewer, then stage or publish only under the task's exact authority. Account access does not grant publication authority.

Apply the maintained [Learn, Do, Teach method](https://github.com/dennisyu/local-service-spotlight-skills/blob/main/docs/agent-setup/LEARN-DO-TEACH.md)
